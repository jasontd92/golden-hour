# Coach profile: Chris Hinshaw (Aerobic Capacity)

## 1. Identity & credentials
- Founder of aerobiccapacity.com. CrossFit named him its Subject Matter Expert in Aerobic Capacity in February 2016, and he teaches the one-day CrossFit Aerobic Capacity specialty course (Prehab Guys ep. 107 show notes, 2022; BarBend course relaunch coverage, 2022).
- Former All-American swimmer and professional triathlete. He finished 10 Ironmans, 7 of them in Kona, and placed 2nd at the Hawaii Ironman World Championship (Prehab Guys, 2022; BOXROX, Riley, 2015).
- Endurance coach to Rich Froning (from late 2014), Mat Fraser, Jason Khalipa (from 2013), Katrín Davíðsdóttir and Tia-Clair Toomey (BOXROX 2015, 2016; Morning Chalk Up 2020, 2024).
- His own triathlon-era volume was about 25,000 m swimming, 300–350 mi cycling and 40–50 mi running per week. He says he regrets "never developing and optimizing my entire spectrum of muscle fibers (slow twitch & fast twitch)" (BOXROX, 2015).

## 2. Domain in a hybrid program
Monostructural aerobic development, mainly running: fatigue-factor testing, a range of interval "gears", sustainable pacing, and recovery between efforts.

## 3. Core philosophy & mindset
- Endurance is specific to each movement and each intensity: "endurance is looking at a movement, and every movement is unique" (Two-Brain Radio, 2019). People "are not realizing that every intensity within that movement is unique" (ibid).
- Gears, not one distance: "We are not targeting one specific distance to be good at." "It's always important to develop an arsenal of gears for your running" (BOXROX, 2016). Fraser trained "13 different intensities, everything from ten-second sprints all the way up to walking" (Two-Brain Radio, 2019; MCU, 2020).
- Moderation: "The key is moderation." "You can't train a Crossfit athlete like a triathlete" (BOXROX, 2016).
- Opportunity cost: "Ask yourself, Is this workout in the highest and the best use of my time?" (aerobiccapacity.com).
- Precision: "The lesson was that one second matters a lot." "If he's not training a specific intensity, then he's not prepared for it" (MCU, 2020).
- Recovery is the measure: "A major metric of aerobic fitness is the ability to recover." "If you are more aerobically fit than a competitor, you will recover faster between events" (MCU, 2020).
- "The thing about running is that you can easily measure your improvements" (BOXROX, 2016).

## 4. Governing principles
1. **Aerobic capacity shows up as how much speed you lose as distance grows.** He calls this the fatigue factor (FF): how fast average speed drops with distance. Many CrossFit athletes do well up to about 2 minutes and then fade because their aerobic system is weak (BOXROX, 2015).
2. **Diagnose by comparing a short max effort with a longer one.** "You take your 400 meter time and your 1 mile time and look at the relationship between the two" (aerobiccapacity.com). Elite runners slow about 6–8%, and general fitness enthusiasts average 21–22% (aerobiccapacity.com). Elsewhere he gives world-class runners as about 6% per doubling of distance and his target for CrossFit athletes as 20–21% between the 400 and the mile (BOXROX, 2015).
3. **The FF is the exponent in Riegel's formula:** t2 = t1 × (d2/d1)^(1+FF). Elite CrossFit athletes are about 1.215, i.e. 21.5% (MCU, Halpin, 2024, quoting Hinshaw).
4. **A large FF means an aerobic limiter, not a speed problem.** Froning's limiter was "the inability to transport and utilize oxygen (cardiorespiratory endurance) while running" (BOXROX, 2015).
5. **Training is individual and driven by test data.** Pacing is "based on your fatigue factor" and "personalized" from "your latest performance testing data" (aerobiccapacity.com). "An individualized training plan considers the event distance, but (also) focuses on the athlete's strengths and weaknesses" (MCU, Beers, 2023).
6. **Train many intensities.** Every intensity "fatigues those muscle fibers" in its own way (Two-Brain Radio, 2019). His stimulus chart lists aerobic threshold, lactate threshold, VO2max, speed endurance and strength endurance (aerobiccapacity.com).
7. **Aerobic volume should complement strength, not interfere with it.** Create "complementary volume versus interfering volume"; "nobody has been able to define how much is too much to create this interference effect" (Two-Brain Radio, 2019). Endurance does not hurt strength, and "in many cases the reverse is true as long as the endurance work is done in moderation" (BOXROX, 2016).
8. **Breathing is trainable.** "Weightlifters and gymnasts generally have weak intercostal and diaphragm muscles." Phase one strengthens the breathing muscles (Fraser used POWERbreathe nightly). Phase two is "a reliable and predictable breathing pattern when moving." "Once the lungs are up to par, the door opens wide for programming in new ways" (MCU, 2020).
9. **Sustainable pace is specific to each movement and is learned by feel.** "Finding your sustainable pace is very difficult because there's no formula." "Every movement in CrossFit has its own sustainable pace" (MCU, 2020).
10. **Similar movements carry over.** "We do so many similar types of movements that we get this carry-over effect into other movements" (Two-Brain Radio, 2019).

## 5. Concrete prescriptions & parameters
**Frequency and volume**
- The aerobiccapacity.com Running program describes **2 weekly workouts**, with an add-on "General Endurance and General Speed" program for building volume once the base is in place (aerobiccapacity.com). BOXROX (2016) reports **3–4 running workouts per week** on his site at that time [SECONDARY].
- Athletes typically commit like this: "I'll dedicate two workouts a week" (Two-Brain Radio, 2019).
- His CrossFit athletes "don't put in a lot of volume" (BOXROX, 2016).
- Froning reportedly ran no "more than 6500 meters per week" (Fitness Volt, Smith, 2021, paraphrasing the Power Project podcast) [SECONDARY, not verified against the episode].

**Time domains tested** (BOXROX, 2016): recovery jog (90-minute pace), 10k (about 40 min), 5k (about 20 min), 1 mile (5–6 min) and 400 m. "400 m speed in Crossfit is critical!"

**Named sessions** (verbatim structures)
- **Hop Scotch** (aerobic threshold): a continuous ladder of easy running at 200/400/600/800/1000/800/600/400/200 m, each followed by a 100 m sprint. That makes 5,900 m with 9 sprints. The easy pace is self-chosen (anything from a fast walk to moderate). Sprints are run at 97–98% so form holds (BOXROX, 2016).
- **Bombolini**: 3 sets of 500 m fast, 200 m recovery jog and 100 m sprint, with no rest inside a set and 5 min between sets. The 500 m is faster than mile-PR pace but slower than 400-PR pace. The 100 m is at or faster than 400-PR pace (BOXROX, 2016).
- **Froning, 13 Jan 2015** (6,900 m): 3 rounds of 600 m (2:04), 400 m (1:23), 300 m (1:02) and 200 m (0:41), with rests of 2:00, 1:30, 1:00 and 0:30, and an easy 800 m jog between rounds (BOXROX, 2015). The rest shrinks as the reps shorten, and the rest is roughly equal to the work.
- **Davíðsdóttir, Nov 2013**: 2 sets of 1000 m (4:32–4:37), 800 m (3:38–3:41), 500 m (2:16–2:18) and 300 m (64–65 s). Recoveries are 200 m jogs, 200 m walks after the 500 m and 300 m, and 2 min between sets. "It is important that you control your pace in the first 1000m" (BOXROX, 2015).
- **Fraser**: 3 rounds of 3 × 600 m at about 6:00/mi pace (5,400 m). He hit a 170 kg clean & jerk PR about 3 hours later (BOXROX, 2016).

**Stimulus zones** (aerobiccapacity.com, paraphrased)
- Aerobic threshold: steady and moderate.
- Lactate threshold: longer intervals, short rest.
- VO2max: shorter, faster intervals with more rest.
- Speed endurance: very low volume, reps under 60 s, full recovery.
- Strength endurance: low volume, explosive.

**Pacing precision:** running intervals even 1 s too fast forced much longer recoveries later (Fraser track session, July 2017; MCU, 2020).

## 6. Progression, testing & measurement
- **Baseline:** a max 400 m and a max 1 mile, then FF = ln(T_mile/T_400) / ln(d_mile/d_400) − 1 [INFERENCE: this is the algebraic rearrangement of the Riegel form in MCU 2024. It reproduces Hinshaw's Froning figures: 60 s and 6:00 give about 28.7–29%, and a 21.5% FF predicts about 5:22].
- **Target:** FF of about 20–22% for CrossFit athletes (BOXROX 2015; aerobiccapacity.com; MCU 2024). The predicted "optimal" mile is the 400 time × (ratio)^1.215.
- **Retest cadence:** in PROJECT PB, weeks 1–2 introduce intervals, weeks 3–4 introduce RPE and lead into a **one-mile test**, then weeks 5–12 are an individualized block (MCU, 2023). Pacing updates from "your latest performance testing data" (aerobiccapacity.com).
- **Expected rate of change:** Froning's mile went 6:00 → 5:41 in 12 weeks → 5:24 at 6 months, against a predicted 5:22 (MCU 2024; BOXROX 2016).
- **Recovery as a metric:** how fast an athlete recovers between efforts, and "how fast can you jog and recover?" (MCU 2020; Two-Brain Radio 2019).
- **Rowing:** a reported 1-minute vs 6-minute test with an average 9.5% gap (attributed to the WHOOP Podcast 143 in search summaries) [SECONDARY / UNVERIFIED: the primary page could not be accessed].

## 7. Explicitly rejects / warns against
- Equating endurance with time: "the biggest mistake people make is they don't realize endurance isn't just, oh, I'm going to put in more time" (Two-Brain Radio, 2019).
- Training at one intensity only. Habitual grinding work means "you trained your muscles to hurt and to go slow" (Two-Brain Radio, 2019).
- Training CrossFit athletes like triathletes, i.e. high volume (BOXROX, 2016).
- Interval paces that drift too fast ("one second matters a lot"; MCU, 2020).
- Ignoring breathing mechanics in strength-dominant athletes (MCU, 2020).

## 8. Does not address (boundaries)
- Barbell programming, loading and 5/3/1 structure. He gives only the anecdote that strength rose alongside running (BOXROX, 2016).
- Gymnastics and metcon design.
- Heart-rate zones or an 80/20 intensity split. None was found in the sources reviewed. His frame is pace, FF and RPE.
- Bike or ski interval prescriptions. aerobiccapacity.com programs running only and gives FAQ advice on translating pacing to other modalities.
- Nutrition, body composition and sleep beyond the general reference to "good nutrition, good recovery" (Two-Brain Radio, 2019).
- Weekly volume-increase rules (e.g. 10%/week). None was found.

## 9. Stated tensions with other approaches
- With high-volume endurance training: CrossFit athletes are not triathletes and should keep volume moderate (BOXROX, 2016).
- With the view that endurance kills strength: he says the reverse holds with moderation (BOXROX, 2016).
- With low-intensity-dominant models such as Seiler's 80/20: [INFERENCE] his low frequency (about 2 sessions/week), interval-heavy design and pace-based zones differ from polarized, volume-driven endurance training. No direct statement found.
