# Coach profile: Nick Bare (hybrid hypertrophy)

## 1. Identity & credentials
- Nick Bare is a former US Army Infantry Platoon Leader stationed at Fort Hood. He founded Bare Performance Nutrition (BPN) and built it while still serving: "During my entire time in the military, I was building my fitness company" (quoted in Generation Iron, 27 Oct 2026). [SECONDARY for the biographical details, from InsideHook 2023 and Coachway.]
- He calls himself a bodybuilder first: "I love the pump. I am a bodybuilder at my core, but I have truly started to love running" (InsideHook, Charles Thorp, 7 Jul 2023).
- Third-party profiles report a 2:48 marathon PR, 100-mile ultras and bodybuilding shows. [SECONDARY, from social media and not verified.]
- Books: *25 Hours a Day: Going One More to Get What You Want* and *Go One More: Find the Clarity to Make Intentional, Life-Changing Choices* (BenBella, 24 Jun 2025, per publisher listings). I could not access the full text of either.
- Programs: Hybrid Athlete 1.0 and 2.0, the Hybrid Athlete Challenge, The Hybrid Cut and Go One More Daily, all sold through the Playbook app. **These are paywalled.** The program pages returned HTTP 403 to my fetches, so the program details below come from public summaries and are flagged as such.

## 2. Domain in a hybrid program
He is the program's voice for building and keeping muscle (bodybuilding-style hypertrophy) alongside steady running, arranged so neither side cannibalises the other.

## 3. Core philosophy & mindset
- **Go One More.** "It is not just a mentality for the workouts, it's a way to be in your career or with your family" (InsideHook 2023). Book lines quoted by reviewers include "We must be willing to endure the process of life" (Goodreads) and "Here's the thing about consistency: you don't have to be born with it" (Substack review, *The Library 006*). [SECONDARY: both are quoted by reviewers and not checked against the book.]
- "The way that you do anything is the way you do everything" (InsideHook 2023).
- He learns by self-experimentation: "The only way that really makes sense for me to learn was through self-experimentation" (InsideHook 2023).
- Hybrid is a lifestyle, not an event: "Hybrid training is a lifestyle, not just event preparation" (YouTube, "How To Train Like A Hybrid Athlete (Running + Lifting)", How To Hybrid ep. 1, listed 3 Nov 2025). [SECONDARY: BriefRead summary.]
- His own goal statement for hybrid: "I wanted to run, and I wanted to do these races, without really losing my size and strength" (InsideHook 2023).
- BPN's house line: "Hybrid training is not random workouts thrown together… Progression is intentional… Recovery is respected" and "Fatigue alone is not progress" (BPN blog, "What Hybrid Training Is, What It Isn't, and Why You Should Do It", 12 Jan 2026, updated 4 Aug 2026; byline unclear). [BPN company voice, not a verified Bare quote.]

## 4. Governing principles
1. **Cut lifting frequency when you add running.** "You can't take your five-day lifting schedule and tack on a five-day running schedule… I'm bringing my strength training down from five days a week to three days a week" (InsideHook 2023).
2. **Minimum weekly dose is 3 strength + 3 low-intensity cardio sessions.** "At least three strength training sessions in per week" plus "three zone two, zone three, lower intensity cardiovascular conditioning… This doesn't have to be running. This could be cycling, this could be swimming, this could even be walking" (YouTube, "Build Your Hybrid Athlete Program (Running + Lifting)", @nickbarefitness, youtube.com/watch?v=3Ke4QbFDH4A; transcript via SozAI, speaker unlabeled). [SECONDARY transcript.]
3. **Progressive overload is the master principle.** "Progressive overload is probably the most important principle that helps facilitate growth." The levers are "exercise selection and style… reps, sets, weight, and rest periods." Without overload, the body gets more efficient but not bigger, stronger or faster (same video).
4. **Keep easy runs easy.** "The biggest mistake I made pursuing the hybrid athlete life was running my easy runs too hard." Also: "Our body benefits tremendously from Zone 2 aerobic conditioning" (InsideHook 2023). BPN's advice is to run easy "below our max-aerobic heart rate for a base-building, easy run" and to start slow and finish fast (BPN, "6 Endurance Training Tips", Austin Myers, "tips from Founder Nick Bare", 7 Sep 2022).
5. **Ramp running gradually.** "We don't want to increase running weekly mileage by more than 10 percent each week, week over week" (Build Your Hybrid Athlete Program video).
6. **Each session leads with a compound lift and trains hard.** "A compound movement leads each workout." Muscle groups are split by day, with training intense enough to push toward exhaustion (BPN, "The 6 Pillars of the Hybrid Build", Austin Myers, 8 Nov 2022, updated 17 Jan 2024).
7. **High volume, varied rep ranges, short rest.** "Nick enjoys training at very high levels of volume," using multiple sets across varied rep ranges with short rest periods (6 Pillars).
8. **Running volume stays below marathon-plan levels during a muscle phase.** Find a weekly mileage baseline that still allows gains in size and strength (6 Pillars). His own running reportedly went from about 40 toward 85 miles per week while he built a marathon (InsideHook 2023).
9. **Separate the domains in time.** In BPN's words: "Sessions are dedicated to a specific domain (strength or endurance)"; "Separate hard runs and heavy lifts by hours (and meals) when possible"; "Place quad-dominant days earlier in the week; hamstring/glute day closer to long runs"; and "Quads affect running more than hamstrings/glutes" (BPN, What Hybrid Training Is, 2026).
10. **Autoregulate.** "Auto-regulation is key to adjusting training based on recovery and upcoming events." After poor sleep, reduce intensity or train a different body part, and move leg days around the long run (How To Hybrid ep. 1, [SECONDARY]). BPN adds: train hard on energised days and lighten the session or rest when run down (6 Pillars). "Swap running for cycling or other aerobic work when recovery demands it" (BPN 2026).
11. **Measure objectively.** "Objective data allows athletes to determine what is fact and what is fiction." Track body weight, bloodwork or DEXA monthly, plus the load on key lifts (6 Pillars).

## 5. Concrete prescriptions & parameters
- **Weekly layout (his free BPN 16-week plan, per Lift Vault's summary):** training Monday to Saturday, with Sunday for "foam rolling, stretching, and an optional walk." There are 5 lifts and 4 runs a week, and "Most days pair a morning run with an evening lifting session." Runs are set by effort. There is a midweek speed run (about 5k pace or tempo) and a Saturday long run, with "the rest of the runs kept easy" (Zone 2). Lifts are set by RPE: the heaviest clean weight for the listed reps. Most lift days add 100 push-ups and 50 pull-ups as filler. [SECONDARY: the sets and reps sit in the spreadsheet, which I did not view.]
- **Hybrid Athlete 1.0, weeks 1–4 (search summary of the paid program):** Mon push strength + run, Tue pull strength + run, Wed lower strength, Thu run + mobility, Fri upper hypertrophy, Sat run, Sun off. That is 4 run days and 5 lift days. [SECONDARY/paywalled.]
- **Same-day order:** the Playbook program notes say "it is recommended to complete the running workouts in the morning and the resistance training workouts in the evening, however you can move the workouts around to fit your specific schedule." [SECONDARY: quoted in a search result, as the page 403'd.] "I love doing my long runs on the weekends right when I wake up" (Generation Iron 2026). He skips the morning run on heavy lower-body PR days (Morning Chalk Up / BarBend, 17 Jan 2024, paraphrasing his 5 Jan 2024 vlog).
- **Hypertrophy sessions (Generation Iron 2026, [SECONDARY], source unnamed):**
  - Typical work is 3–4 sets per exercise.
  - The heavy compound lift is 3×5 (back squat, deadlift), with accessories at 8–12 reps and core at 15–20.
  - The leg day pre-exhausts with leg extensions and curls before squats.
  - Morning Chalk Up 2024 reports a squat at 5×5 @315 after extensions and curls, followed by walking lunges.
- **Weekly volume:** "roughly 10 to 20 hard sets per muscle group weekly, depending on experience." He also distinguishes strength work from hypertrophy work (Nick Bare Podcast ep. 185, "Answering Your Nutrition and Training Questions"). [UNVERIFIED: this came from a search snippet, and the transcript page failed to load twice.]
- **Proximity to failure:** I found no explicit RIR or failure rule from Bare. The closest are "intense enough to push muscles toward exhaustion" (6 Pillars) and AMRAP pull-up sets (Generation Iron). [INFERENCE: hypertrophy sets end near failure, while the heavy compound is RPE-based.]
- **Nutrition:**
  - "Muscle can be built at any point when the rate of muscle protein synthesis outweighs that of muscle protein breakdown." Eat enough total calories and protein for both kinds of adaptation, and track intake (6 Pillars).
  - He eats carb-rich meals around runs, with carbs and sodium before long runs (Morning Chalk Up 2024).
  - BPN also says to increase sodium before training (6 Endurance Tips).

## 6. Progression, testing & measurement
- **Lifts:** add weight when the top of the rep range moves cleanly (16-week plan, Lift Vault).
- **Running:** no more than 10% more per week. The 16-week plan's phases are base (weeks 1–4), added speed and longer Saturdays (5–8), peak mileage with a half-marathon time trial (week 12), then reduced mileage and a 5k time trial (week 16). "There's no formal deload, the taper is built into those last weeks" (Lift Vault summary).
- **Deloads:** I found no fixed deload rule from Bare himself. Recovery is handled by autoregulation and taper.
- **Measurement:** monthly body weight, bloodwork or DEXA, and a log of "the exercises and the reps and the sets, the weights you used," plus pace, heart rate and RPE (6 Pillars; Build video).
- **Training emphasis:** pick a priority goal per block, maintain the other side, and rotate. [SECONDARY: Coachway paraphrase of the Build video.]

## 7. Explicitly rejects / warns against
- Running easy runs too hard (InsideHook 2023).
- Stacking a full lifting week on a full running week (InsideHook 2023).
- Unchanging stimulus: the body adapts and stops growing (Build video).
- Random, exhausting workouts. BPN: "They were exhausting, but they didn't consistently make people stronger or faster." Also "Fatigue alone is not progress" (BPN 2026).
- The myth that running makes you weak, which BPN says ignores other factors (6 Pillars). Muscle loss comes from very high endurance volume, under-fuelling or skipping strength work, not from moderate running (How To Hybrid ep. 1, [SECONDARY]).
- Hard runs and heavy lower-body lifts close together (BPN 2026).

## 8. Does not address (boundaries)
- Gymnastics or calisthenics skill progressions (L-sit, strict pull-up programs), metcons and CrossFit benchmarks.
- Percentage-based barbell programs like 5/3/1. He uses RPE and rep-range progression.
- A formal 80/20 intensity split. He says "mostly easy" with one quality run plus a long run, but gives no percentage.
- Exact interval paces from test results.
- Precise sets, reps and RIR in the paid programs, which I could not verify.

## 9. Stated tensions with other approaches
- None named explicitly in my sources.
- [INFERENCE] Vs high-variety CrossFit metcons: BPN criticises random, fatigue-driven workouts.
- [INFERENCE] Vs low-frequency running models (3 runs/week): his own plans use about 4 runs a week, though his stated minimum is 3 aerobic sessions, any modality.
- [INFERENCE] Vs Wendler-style low assistance: Bare favours high accessory volume.
