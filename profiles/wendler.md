# Coach profile: Jim Wendler / 5/3/1

## 1. Identity & credentials
Jim Wendler is a former competitive powerlifter (elite total in the multiply era) and college football player (University of Arizona), a long-time writer and partner at elitefts, and the author of *5/3/1: The Simplest and Most Effective Training System for Building Raw Strength* (first edition 2009), *Beyond 5/3/1* (2011) and *5/3/1 Forever* (2017). 5/3/1 first appeared publicly in 2009 in T-Nation coverage ("How to Build Pure Strength", by Bryan Krahn, July 2009) [SECONDARY: original article not retrieved]. His current writing lives at jimwendler.com. The books are not freely online, so book-only claims below are marked [SECONDARY] when confirmed only through third-party summaries.

## 2. Domain in a hybrid program
Barbell strength: the main lifts, supplemental volume, assistance, jumps/throws. He also has firm rules on how conditioning and running fit around the lifting.

## 3. Core philosophy & mindset
- Five core values: **"Start too light," "Progress slow," "Use compound movements," "Attempt to set PRs," "Balance"** (Dave Tate relaying Wendler's values, elitefts Table Talk clip, 23 Mar 2019).
- "And if in doubt, start too light." / "Start lighter — this is probably the best advice." (Wendler, "52 Most Common 5/3/1 Questions", elitefts, 2010/2013).
- "In my opinion the more progress you can achieve out of a low TM the better." / "A higher TM does not equal more strength." / "Training is NOT testing." (Wendler, "The Training Max: What You Need to Know", jimwendler.com, undated).
- "Your conditioning is PART of your training and must be accounted for when designing your total program." (Wendler, "Do You Need to Condition?", jimwendler.com, c. 2017 [INFERENCE on date]).
- "You cannot serve two masters." (52 Questions, #32)

## 4. Governing principles
1. **Train off a training max (TM), not your true max.** Original TM = 90% of 1RM: "The 90% rule was the recommended starting point." Later books: "Beyond 5/3/1 may mention 85% or 80% or whatever." (The Training Max, jimwendler.com). The beginner program uses "80-85% of your max" (The 5/3/1 Philosophy for Beginners, jimwendler.com). Forever recommends starting leaders at a TM no higher than 85% [SECONDARY: Lift Vault, Leader & Anchor Cycles].
2. **Start light, progress slowly, and don't chase the TM upward.** If a retest beats your TM math: "Don't recalculate. Keep the path you're on now." (52 Questions, #16). Raising the TM because your max rose "is the wrong thing to do." (The Training Max)
3. **Stalls are fixed by resetting and going lighter, not by grinding.** "Take five steps forward, 2-3 steps back, and repeat." / "Do more or do less — too many workouts in a week sometimes do more harm than good." (52 Questions, #10). The Training Max article shows "5 forward/3 back" resets.
4. **Never grind or miss reps; form gates progression.** "Failure is when the lift cannot be done with correct technique." (52 Questions). If reps don't meet the standard, "YOU DO NOT INCREASE YOUR TRAINING MAX." (5/3/1 for Beginners). "You should never miss reps when running 5/3/1. This includes joker sets." [SECONDARY: Lift Vault, Joker Sets].
5. **The 1+ set is used with judgement, not maxed every week.** "You don't have to max out on the last set all the time." Go all out two or three times per cycle, push the 3s and 5/3/1 weeks, and "leave at least 1 full rep in the tank." (52 Questions, #10–11). Each set has a rep goal, and once it's reached, that's the final rep (#33).
6. **Conditioning is mandatory and is accounted for in the lifting.** Hard conditioning goes on lower-body days, easy conditioning on upper-body days, and "Never do conditioning the day before lower body lifting." (52 Questions, #32, #46).
7. **When you add significant running, lower the TM.** "So once I began my hill running, the first thing I did was lower my training max significantly on my 5/3/1 workout." He also cut lower-body assistance. (Wendler, "Everything You Could Want to Know About Running Hills", collected in 52 Questions, elitefts.)
8. **Adapt gradually to new conditioning.** "Take three weeks to adapt to it." / "You don't have to make the first day into Hell Day." (52 Questions, #30). Combining BBB and conditioning is fine, but not right away (#12).
9. **Assistance stays limited and consistent.** "Limit assistance work to 2-3 exercises and go by feel." "Do them consistently for 8-12 weeks before switching (2-3 cycles)." (52 Questions).
10. **Pick the supplemental volume for the goal and phase, and recover accordingly.** "Generally speaking BBB makes you a bit bigger"; "5x5 is (obviously) easier to recover from"; with BBB "you better recover harder." "As long as your TM is solid, 99% of the time 5x5 @ FSL should be good/strong." (5x5 FSL vs BBB, jimwendler.com).

## 5. Concrete prescriptions & parameters
**Original 4-week wave** (% of TM) [SECONDARY: widely reproduced from the 2009 book, e.g. Lift Vault 5/3/1 spreadsheet]:
- Wk1: 65×5, 75×5, 85×5+
- Wk2: 70×3, 80×3, 90×3+
- Wk3: 75×5, 85×3, 95×1+
- Wk4 deload: 40×5, 50×5, 60×5
- One main lift per session. Four days/week in the original; 3-day rotations exist.

**5/3/1 Forever: 3-week cycles + 7th Week Protocol** [SECONDARY: Lift Vault]:
- Cycles run weeks 1–3 without the week-4 deload. A typical block is 2 Leader cycles (6 wk) → 7th week → 1 Anchor cycle (3 wk) → 7th week.
- **Leader:** lower main-lift intensity, more supplemental volume, less assistance (example: 5's PRO + 5×5 FSL, assistance 25–50 reps per category).
- **Anchor:** higher intensity (PR sets / Jokers), less supplemental work, more assistance (example: 5/3/1 with PR sets + 3×5 FSL, assistance 50–100 reps).
- The jimwendler.com FSL/BBB post links leader-phase jumps/throws to being "better prepared for the Anchor phase."
- **7th Week deload:** 40/50/60% TM × 5.
- **7th Week TM test:** 70×5, 80×5, 90×5, then 100% TM for reps. "Fewer than 3 reps means the training max is too high and should come down." [SECONDARY: Lift Vault]

**Variants** [SECONDARY unless noted]:
- **5's PRO:** all three working sets ×5, no AMRAP (Lift Vault glossary).
- **PR set:** the 1+ set, taken for a rep PR.
- **Joker sets:** after the top set on a strong day, +5–10% for the same or fewer reps (usually 1–3). They are earned, the exception rather than the rule, and you never miss reps on them (Lift Vault, Joker Sets). Beyond 5/3/1 introduced FSL and Jokers [SECONDARY].

**Supplemental templates** [SECONDARY for percentages]:
- **FSL:** 5×5 (or 3–5×5–8) at the first working-set %, which is 65/70/75%. The beginner program uses 5×5 FSL (jimwendler.com, primary).
- **SSL:** second-set %.
- **BBB:** 5×10 at ~50–70% TM.
- **BBS:** 10×5 at FSL %.
- **Widowmaker:** 1×20.
- "Not every 5/3/1 program has a supplemental lift" (5/3/1 Forever excerpt, jimwendler.com).

**Assistance** (5/3/1 for Beginners, jimwendler.com, primary): every session, one exercise each from **push, pull, single-leg/core**, "50-100 total reps" each, with any set scheme.
- Push: dips, push-ups, DB presses, triceps.
- Pull: chins/pull-ups, inverted rows, rows, face pulls, band pull-aparts, curls.
- Single-leg/core: ab work, back raises, lunges, step-ups, split squats, KB swings and snatches.

**Jumps/throws:** "10-15 total box jumps or med ball throws before lifting" (5/3/1 for Beginners). 10–15 contacts per session alongside BBB (FSL vs BBB post).

**Beginner 3-day schedule** (primary):
- Mon: Squat + Bench
- Wed: Deadlift + Press
- Fri: Bench + Squat
- Each lift is 5/3/1 + 5×5 FSL, with conditioning on Tue/Thu/Sat/Sun.

**Conditioning:**
- "3 days/week (sled, prowler, run hills)" (52 Questions, #3).
- Prowler: easy (10×40 yd, 15–20 min) and medium sessions "can be performed between training days or after bench press or press workouts"; the hard session "after you squat and deadlift" (Three Prowler Workouts, jimwendler.com).
- Easy conditioning is "a way to zone out and relax. A way to discipline yourself to adhere to a schedule." (Do You Need to Condition?)

**Running with 5/3/1:** Wendler ran hills 4 days/week after lifting and lowered his TM to do it (Running Hills essay). Hunter Richardson's elitefts "5/3/1 and Run" (16 Apr 2022, not by Wendler) uses tempo/interval + moderate + LSD runs. It adds: "Supplemental sets are optional and depend upon the athlete's experience and conditioning," and if you're not conditioned for both, start with original 5/3/1 and no supplemental work [SECONDARY: not Wendler's own words].

## 6. Progression, testing & measurement
- **TM increases** per cycle: +5 lb upper body (press, bench), +10 lb lower body (squat, deadlift) [SECONDARY: original book, widely reproduced].
- **Forever progression:** raise the TM each cycle as long as the 7th-week TM test passes [SECONDARY].
- **Estimated 1RM** from rep sets: weight × reps × 0.0333 + weight. Wendler tracks rep PRs at a given weight rather than testing maxes [SECONDARY: original book].
- **Reset rule:** if you fail the minimum reps, or the TM test gives fewer than 3 reps, lower the TM (commonly ~10%) and work back up [SECONDARY]. After long runs of progress, "take the weights from the second or third cycle and repeat." (52 Questions)
- **Testing:** "Training is NOT testing." If you test, do it after the prescribed 5/3/1 sets: "rest and then hit a heavier weight" (52 Questions, #37) with "a little bigger than 5% jumps."

## 7. Explicitly rejects / warns against
- Starting heavy or inflating the TM: "A higher TM does not equal more strength."
- Recalculating the TM after a good test.
- Missing reps and grinding with broken form.
- Maxing every 1+ set.
- Too much assistance, too many exercises, or changing exercises too often.
- Adding hard conditioning too fast: "too much conditioning too soon" (#32).
- Conditioning the day before lower-body lifting.
- "Don't buy into the 'run around until you are out of breath' and expect it to work for long." (Do You Need to Condition?)
- Training that measures a workout "by how much you threw up" (Darkthrone or Metallica, jimwendler.com).

## 8. Does not address (boundaries)
- Run pacing, aerobic zones, 80/20 distribution, or endurance periodization. He gives no running paces.
- Gymnastics skill progressions (pull-up and L-sit protocols beyond assistance rep totals).
- Metcon design.
- Nutrition specifics in the sources reviewed.
- Assault bike prescriptions. His examples use the prowler, sled and hills.

## 9. Stated tensions with other approaches
- The concurrent-training tension is stated directly: "you cannot serve two masters" (#32). When conditioning rises, lifting volume or TM comes down (Running Hills essay).
- Against high-frequency max-out training: "too many workouts in a week sometimes do more harm than good" (#10).
- [INFERENCE] Conflicts with CrossFit-style randomized, intense metcons placed before leg days, and with run-volume models that put hard running the day before squat/deadlift.
